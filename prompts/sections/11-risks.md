# Section: Risks & Mitigations

> **Adapt to the project type.** For software: technical, dependency, scaling risks.
> For physical businesses/events: logistics, legal, supplier, community risks.

## What to Capture
- For each risk: description, category, impact, likelihood, mitigation strategy
- Categories: technical, resource, timeline, budget, security, market, legal, logistics, other

## Questions to Ask

### For all projects:
1. What's the biggest thing that could go wrong?
2. What happens if you can't find users/customers/attendees in the first month?
3. What's your plan if this takes 3x longer than expected?

### For digital products (add):
4. Are there technical unknowns you're worried about?
5. What if a key dependency or service gets discontinued?

### For physical businesses/events (add):
4. What if a key supplier, venue, or partner falls through?
5. Are there legal or regulatory risks? (Permits, insurance, liability, compliance)
6. What if demand is much higher or lower than expected?

## Quality Tips
- Every project has at least 3 risks. If you can't find them, you're not looking hard enough.
- The biggest risk is usually not technical — it's "nobody wants this." Validate demand early.
- For each risk, have a concrete mitigation, not just "we'll deal with it."
- Categorize risks: technical (can we build it?), market (do people want it?), resource (do we have the capacity?), legal (are we allowed?).
- Revisit risks regularly. New ones emerge, old ones get resolved.

## Common Mistakes
- Only listing technical risks (market and resource risks are usually bigger)
- Vague mitigations ("we'll be careful" is not a plan)
- Not having a "kill switch" — at what point do you stop and reassess?
- Ignoring dependency/vendor risks (what if a key supplier, tool, or service disappears?)
- Not considering the risk of success (what if demand exceeds your capacity?)
