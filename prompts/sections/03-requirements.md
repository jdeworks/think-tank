# Section: Requirements

## What to Capture
- Functional requirements (what the system DOES) with priority: must/should/nice
- Non-functional requirements (performance, accessibility, reliability)
- Constraints (budget, timeline, team size, tech limitations)
- Assumptions (what you're taking for granted)

## Questions to Ask
1. What are the 3-5 things this MUST do on day one? (Must-haves)
2. What would make it great but isn't essential for launch? (Should-haves)
3. Any dream features for the future? (Nice-to-haves)
4. Are there hard constraints? (Budget, deadline, team size, specific tech required)
5. What are you assuming about your users? (Internet speed, device, tech skills)

## Quality Tips
- Use MoSCoW prioritization: Must, Should, Could, Won't
- Every "must" you add delays launch. Be ruthless about what's truly essential.
- Non-functional requirements (speed, uptime, accessibility) are often forgotten but critical.
- Constraints are your friends — they force creative, focused solutions.
- Assumptions should be stated explicitly so they can be validated.

## Common Mistakes
- Making everything a "must-have" (if everything is priority 1, nothing is)
- Forgetting accessibility (it's much harder to add later)
- Not considering offline scenarios for web apps
- Assuming users have fast internet or modern devices
- Ignoring mobile users (>50% of web traffic)
