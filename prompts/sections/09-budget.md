# Section: Budget & Resources

> **Adapt to the project type.** For software: dev effort, hosting, services.
> For physical businesses/events: rent, equipment, staffing, supplies, venue.

## What to Capture
- Effort to build or set up (person-hours, person-weeks, or prep time)
- Ongoing operational costs (infrastructure, rent, supplies, subscriptions)
- Third-party/external costs (services, vendors, licenses, tools)
- Total estimate (one-time setup + monthly recurring)
- Funding model (self-funded, sponsors, revenue, grants)

## Questions to Ask

### For all projects:
1. Is this a solo effort or do you have a team?
2. What's your monthly budget for running this?
3. What's the maximum you'd spend before deciding this isn't worth it?

### For digital products (add):
4. Are you building this yourself or hiring developers?
5. Any paid services you already use or plan to use? (Hosting, APIs, auth, email)

### For physical businesses/events (add):
4. What are the major setup costs? (Equipment, renovation, permits, initial inventory)
5. What are the recurring costs? (Rent, staff, supplies, insurance)
6. How will this be funded? (Self-funded, sponsors, ticket sales, grants?)

## Quality Tips
- Always calculate Total Cost of Ownership: setup + ongoing operations + your time.
- Free tiers and low-cost options exist for almost everything. Start there.
- Your own time IS a cost, even if you're not paying yourself.
- Plan for what happens at 10x scale — do costs grow linearly or exponentially?
- For recurring projects (events, services): calculate per-occurrence cost, not just monthly.

## Common Mistakes
- Not accounting for ongoing maintenance or operations time
- Forgetting recurring costs (subscriptions, supplies, rent, renewals)
- Choosing "enterprise" tools or spaces for a small project
- Not setting a budget cap (spend $X/month max, then reassess)
- Ignoring the cost of your own time
- No funding plan — assuming costs will just work out
